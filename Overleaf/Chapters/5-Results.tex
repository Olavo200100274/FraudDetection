\section{Baseline Model Comparison (No Imbalance Strategy)}
\label{sec:baseline_results}

The baseline addresses RQ1 by establishing a reference for each model without resampling or cost-sensitive weighting. The four classical supervised models use Optuna TPE with 50 trials and five-fold stratified validation; FT-Transformer uses 50 trials and one internal DEV holdout; and OCSVM uses a fixed configuration without hyperparameter search. Every TEST operating point uses a threshold selected within DEV. Section~\ref{sec:threshold_sensitivity} subsequently applies alternative validation-derived thresholds to the saved baseline TEST scores. For FT-Transformer, the validation model used to reconstruct those study-specific thresholds differs from the original early-stopping run, as reported below.

\section{Threshold Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:threshold_sensitivity}

This section addresses RQ2 by applying the four rules introduced in Section~\ref{sec:threshold_study} --- fixed $\tau = 0.5$, max-$F_1$, max-$F_2$, and the lowest validation threshold satisfying $\text{Precision} \geq 0.5$ --- to the saved baseline TEST scores. For the classical models and OCSVM, thresholds are medians from five validation folds. For FT-Transformer, a separate validation model is fitted for the baseline-selected number of epochs on the same deterministic internal split to reconstruct the four thresholds; the saved final-model TEST scores remain unchanged. Consequently, the FT-Transformer max-$F_2$ entry in this section is study-specific and differs from its original baseline operating point, particularly on ULB (0.820 vs.\ 0.807). TEST labels remain excluded from threshold selection throughout. The analysis reports changes in $F_2$, $F_1$, recall, alert rate, and model ordering.

On ULB, threshold choice has a moderate effect. Logistic Regression gains most among the supervised models: $F_2$ rises from 0.664 under the fixed rule to 0.799 under max-$F_2$, a 20\% relative improvement. On BAF, the fixed $\tau = 0.5$ rule yields $F_2 < 0.05$ for every supervised model, and Random Forest produces almost no positive predictions ($F_2 = 0.001$, Recall $= 0.0005$). Few cases receive scores above 0.5, so the default threshold misses most positives. For the three leading models, max-$F_2$ produces approximately 7.5-fold, 8.5-fold, and 9.1-fold increases for LGBM (0.042 to 0.316), CatBoost (0.037 to 0.314), and FT-Transformer (0.035 to 0.317), respectively. The max-$F_1$ rule has lower recall than max-$F_2$, while the precision-constrained rule raises fewer alerts. OCSVM cannot satisfy the validation precision target, represented by $\tau=\infty$, and therefore produces no TEST alerts under that rule. Moving from fixed to max-$F_2$ raises the supervised alert rate from at most 0.07\% to approximately 3--4\%, making the review-workload trade-off explicit.

Threshold rules also reorder models on BAF. LGBM has the highest fixed-rule $F_2$ (0.042), FT-Transformer the highest max-$F_2$ value (0.317), CatBoost the highest max-$F_1$ value (0.274), and FT-Transformer the highest precision-constrained value (0.070). Together with the ULB changes, this shows that model selection based on a threshold-dependent metric must specify the operating rule. On BAF, the observed within-model $F_2$ changes are substantially larger than the baseline differences among the leading supervised models; this conclusion is limited to threshold-dependent metrics and does not affect PR-AUC.

\section{FT-Transformer Attention Diagnostics}
\label{sec:attention_diagnostics}

This section addresses the attention component of RQ5. The exploratory diagnostic describes the final-layer, head-averaged \texttt{[CLS]} attention distribution; it is not a faithful feature-attribution method and does not identify the cause of predictive performance. The analysis examines overall concentration through normalised entropy, \texttt{[CLS]} self-attention, the balance between categorical and numerical tokens, and selected case-level matrices.

The quantities are computed on all $200\,000$ BAF Base TEST samples using the baseline FT-Transformer configuration ($\tau = 0.058$). The model operates on $31$ tokens: $25$ numerical features, $5$ categorical features, and the \texttt{[CLS]} classification token. Only the final transformer layer is aggregated, and PyTorch averages the weights across attention heads.

\subsection{Aggregate Attention Distribution}

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/baf/attention_aggregate.png}
\captionof{figure}[Mean per-token attention on BAF Base (FT-Transformer)]{Mean attention weight per feature token aggregated over all $200\,000$ BAF Base test samples. Categorical features are shown in orange; numerical features in blue. Error bars represent standard deviation across samples. A perfectly uniform distribution would assign $1/31 \approx 3.2\%$ to each token.}
\label{fig:attention_aggregate}
\end{minipage}

Four statistics summarise the aggregate distribution shown in Figure~\ref{fig:attention_aggregate}. The normalised entropy is $H_{\text{norm}}=0.976$: the observed entropy (3.352 nats) is 97.6\% of the theoretical maximum for 31 tokens ($\ln 31=3.434$ nats). Mean \texttt{[CLS]} self-attention is 3.1\%, close to the uniform reference of $1/31\approx3.2\%$. Mean attention to categorical and numerical tokens is 4.0\% and 3.1\%, respectively (ratio $1.28\times$). The three largest mean token weights are \texttt{device\_os} (6.2\%), \texttt{housing\_status} (5.0\%), and \texttt{employment\_status} (3.8\%); the first two, but not the third, also appear among the three leading LGBM and CatBoost SHAP features. The final-layer average is therefore diffuse rather than strongly concentrated, although head-specific or earlier-layer structure may be hidden by averaging.

\label{fig:attention_heatmap_fp}
\end{minipage}

The final-layer average for the selected false-positive case (Figure~\ref{fig:attention_heatmap_fp}) contains broadly similar values, with limited elevation for \texttt{device\_os} and \texttt{housing\_status}. The selected false-negative cases in Appendix~\ref{appendixB} (p.~\pageref{appendixB}) show a similar qualitative last-layer pattern. These examples illustrate diffuse attention in the inspected cases but do not determine how earlier layers, individual heads, residual paths, or feed-forward blocks contributed to the final prediction.

The attention diagnostic supports one limited conclusion: the final-layer, head-averaged \texttt{[CLS]} attention is diffuse for the fitted baseline model. It does not show that all available signal has been exhausted, that no feature subset could improve prediction, or that the shared rounded PR-AUC of 0.180 is an information-theoretic ceiling. The CatBoost--FT-Transformer parity is therefore reported as an empirical result on BAF Base, while the cause of that parity remains unresolved. Likewise, the 1.28 categorical-to-numerical mean ratio is descriptive and is not interpreted as evidence of superior categorical processing.

\section{Synthesis and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion}

The largest observed within-model changes in threshold-dependent $F_2$ arise from the decision rule, especially on BAF; PR-AUC, by definition, is unaffected by this choice. No supervised model dominates every criterion. CatBoost has the highest ULB baseline PR-AUC, while LGBM, CatBoost, and FT-Transformer have closely grouped BAF point estimates with overlapping marginal bootstrap intervals. Random Forest has a distinct BAF transfer profile whose advantage depends on the Variant and metric. LGBM offers a favourable measured performance--cost--TreeSHAP combination. The factorial analysis shows that the tested imbalance interventions provide limited PR-AUC improvement for the fitted gradient-boosted models. For FT-Transformer, Class Weights remains close to baseline on BAF but not on ULB, while the 41\% SMOTE reduction is conditional on a non-categorical-aware resampling path. SHAP supports top-feature-set agreement among LR, LGBM, and CatBoost; FT-Transformer SHAP comparisons are excluded because of the gradient limitation. The attention analysis records diffuse final-layer, head-averaged attention, but neither proves a feature-space ceiling nor explains the SMOTE result.

\subsection*{Threats to Validity}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Internal:} the hyperparameter tuning budget is fixed at $50$ Optuna trials per supervised model, and a single outer holdout split is used instead of repeated random subsampling. The FT-Transformer search covers more dimensions than the classical-model searches, so the same trial count may under-explore its potential.
  \item \textbf{Validation protocol:} the classical models use five-fold inner validation, whereas FT-Transformer uses one internal holdout. The shared TEST partition remains untouched, but threshold dispersion and model-selection variance are not estimated on the same basis.
  \item \textbf{Resampling representation:} SMOTE operates on one-hot columns for the classical models and on concatenated numerical values and ordinal category codes for FT-Transformer. Subsequent integer conversion of synthetic FT categorical values is a confound, so the observed SMOTE interaction cannot be attributed solely to architecture.
  \item \textbf{External:} ULB features are PCA-anonymised, limiting interpretability and the real-world applicability of ULB-specific findings. The BAF suite is synthetically generated, so fraud patterns may not fully reflect the complexity of production fraud streams, where label noise, adversarial adaptation, and operational feedback loops are present.
  \item \textbf{Construct:} PR-AUC is sensitive to the class prior, so absolute comparisons across ULB, BAF Base, and BAF Variants with different fraud rates require caution. ROC-AUC and operating-point metrics provide complementary views but do not remove all transfer confounding.
  \item \textbf{Interpretability:} FT-Transformer GradientExplainer attributions are unreliable for categorical embedding indices and are excluded from cross-model conclusions. The attention diagnostic averages heads in the final layer and may hide head- or layer-specific structure; attention is not a faithful output attribution.
  \item \textbf{Statistical:} the bootstrap CIs assume that the holdout test set is representative of the underlying distribution and quantify test-sample uncertainty conditional on one fitted model. They do not capture split, seed, training, or tuning variability, and overlapping marginal intervals are not a paired test of model differences.
  \item \textbf{Cross-dataset:} ULB and BAF have incompatible feature spaces, which prevents cross-architecture, cross-dataset transfer. The cross-domain study is restricted to the BAF internal transfer setting, which, while controlled, represents a narrower robustness evaluation than matched feature spaces across independent datasets would provide.
\end{itemize}
