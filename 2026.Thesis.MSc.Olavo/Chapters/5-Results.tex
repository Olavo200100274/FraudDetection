\subsection{Baseline Model Comparison (No Imbalance Strategy)}
\label{sec:baseline_results}

(scoring\,=\,PR-AUC) \emph{without} any imbalance handling strategy.

$F_2$-maximising threshold~($\tau$) obtained from inner validation
(median across folds).
The same trained models and probability scores are later reused for
the threshold sensitivity analysis (Section~\ref{sec:threshold_sensitivity}),
where alternative threshold strategies are applied \emph{without retraining}.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
On ULB~2013 (Tables~\ref{tab:baseline_ulb}--\ref{tab:ops_ulb},
Figure~\ref{fig:pr_curves_ulb}), CatBoost achieves the highest PR-AUC (0.886),
followed closely by RF (0.871) and LGBM (0.865), while LR lags behind (0.742).
LGBM stands out operationally with the lowest FP/TP ratio (0.12) and
lowest alert rate (0.16\,\%), meaning fewer false alarms per true positive.
OCSVM performs substantially worse (PR-AUC\,=\,0.335), confirming that
unsupervised anomaly detection is a weak baseline when labelled data is available.
All bootstrap 95\,\% CIs for tree-based models overlap,
suggesting the performance differences among them are not statistically significant
at this sample size.

On BAF~Base (Tables~\ref{tab:baseline_baf}--\ref{tab:ops_baf},
Figure~\ref{fig:pr_curves_baf_base}),
the same ranking holds---CatBoost (0.179), LGBM (0.173), RF (0.154), LR (0.143)---but
all PR-AUC values are dramatically lower than on ULB~2013.
Three factors explain this gap:
(i)~the ULB features are PCA-transformed principal components that already
capture the most discriminative variance;
(ii)~BAF is a synthetic benchmark (CTGAN-generated) specifically designed
to be a harder, more realistic task;
and (iii)~BAF contains five categorical features that, once one-hot encoded,
produce sparse binary columns that dilute the signal for linear models.
Precision is uniformly low ($\leq$~0.14 for all supervised models),
resulting in FP/TP ratios of 6--7 (vs.\ $<$~1 on ULB).
The best Recall@$k$ is only 0.155 (CatBoost, $k{=}1000$),
reflecting the difficulty of concentrating fraud at the top of a
one-million-row ranking.
OCSVM collapses almost entirely (PR-AUC\,=\,0.019), producing an
alert rate of 7.3\,\% with 37.7 false positives per true positive---
effectively unusable in an operational setting.

FT-Transformer results will be added once that model is implemented.

\subsection{Threshold Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:threshold_sensitivity}

This section investigates how the choice of decision threshold strategy
affects all downstream metrics and, critically, whether it changes model rankings.
The four strategies defined in Section~\ref{sec:threshold_study} of the Methodology are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Fixed ($\tau=0.5$):} na\"ive baseline.
  \item \textbf{max-$F_1$:} threshold maximising $F_1$ on validation folds (median across folds).
  \item \textbf{max-$F_2$:} threshold maximising $F_2$ on validation folds (median across folds)~--- the primary analysis criterion.
  \item \textbf{Precision\,$\geq$\,0.5:} lowest threshold ensuring $\text{Precision} \geq 0.5$ on validation folds (median across folds).
\end{enumerate}

All strategies select $\tau$ exclusively on inner validation folds;
the same $\tau$ is then applied to the holdout test set.
No retraining is performed~--- the same probability scores from the baseline are reused.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
The threshold sensitivity analysis
(Tables~\ref{tab:threshold_ulb_f2}--\ref{tab:threshold_ulb_alert})
reveals three key findings on ULB~2013.

\textit{Fixed $\tau=0.5$ is catastrophic for low-calibrated models.}
Logistic Regression, whose optimal thresholds lie around $\tau\approx0.048$,
loses $-$21\,pp in recall (from 0.847 under max-$F_2$ to 0.633 under Fixed~0.5)
and $-$15\,pp in $F_2$ (0.809~$\to$~0.664).
Gradient-boosted models (LGBM, CatBoost) are more naturally calibrated
and show smaller drops with Fixed~0.5, but still lose 3--4\,pp in $F_2$.
RF is the most robust model to threshold choice:
its $F_2$ spans only 8\,pp (0.787--0.870) compared to 15\,pp for LR and 13\,pp for CatBoost.

\textit{Model rankings shift with the threshold strategy.}
Under max-$F_1$, CatBoost ranks \#2 with $F_2=0.865$,
but under Prec\,$\geq$\,0.5, it drops to \#4 ($F_2=0.733$)~---
overtaken by both LR ($F_2=0.744$) and LGBM ($F_2=0.755$).
RF consistently ranks \#1 across all four strategies,
suggesting it is the safest default choice when threshold tuning resources are limited.

\textit{The Prec\,$\geq$\,0.5 constraint does not always transfer to the test set.}
CatBoost achieves only 0.423 precision on test under this strategy
(despite the constraint being satisfied on validation folds),
highlighting the inherent gap between validation-tuned thresholds and holdout performance.
This underscores the importance of reporting threshold-transfer fidelity,
not just optimised-fold precision.

Overall, these results confirm that threshold selection is not a post-hoc afterthought
but a first-class design decision: it can shift reported $F_2$ by up to 15\,pp
and alter the ``best model'' ranking.
This motivates the max-$F_2$ strategy adopted throughout this thesis
as the primary threshold rule for fraud detection.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
The BAF threshold sensitivity analysis
(Tables~\ref{tab:threshold_baf_f2}--\ref{tab:threshold_baf_alert})
amplifies the patterns observed on ULB but with starker contrasts.

\textit{Fixed $\tau=0.5$ is catastrophic on BAF.}
With a 1.1\,\% fraud rate, model scores rarely exceed 0.5.
CatBoost retains only 2.5\,\% recall under Fixed~0.5 ($F_2=0.031$),
and RF is virtually non-functional ($F_2=0.001$, recall\,$<$\,0.1\,\%).
This confirms that the default threshold is entirely unsuitable for
imbalanced datasets where calibrated probabilities lie far below~0.5.

\textit{max-$F_2$ provides the best recall--$F_2$ trade-off.}
CatBoost achieves $F_2=0.318$ (recall\,=\,0.473) under max-$F_2$,
roughly 6$\times$ higher than max-$F_1$ in recall terms.
However, this comes at an alert rate of 3.81\,\%, compared to 1.41\,\% for max-$F_1$.
In a production setting with 200\,000 daily applications, max-$F_2$
would flag ${\sim}7{,}600$ for review versus ${\sim}2{,}800$ under max-$F_1$.

\textit{Prec\,$\geq$\,0.5 is effectively infeasible on BAF.}
All supervised models produce recall below 5\,\% and $F_2$ below 0.06
under the Prec\,$\geq$\,0.5 constraint.
The required threshold ($\tau\approx0.39$--$0.46$) is too aggressive
for a dataset where even the best model assigns a median fraud score of only~0.051.
OCSVM produces zero predictions (Prec\,$\geq$\,0.5 yields $\tau=\infty$).

\textit{OCSVM is not viable for BAF.}
Even under the best threshold strategy (max-$F_2$), OCSVM achieves
$F_2=0.081$ with 7.32\,\% alert rate~--- substantially worse than any
supervised model across all strategies.

These findings reinforce the conclusion from ULB: threshold selection is a first-class
design decision that can shift $F_2$ by over 25\,pp on BAF, and the
max-$F_2$ strategy remains the most appropriate for fraud detection.

Tables~\ref{tab:factorial_ulb_prauc}--\ref{tab:factorial_ulb_f2}
and Tables~\ref{tab:factorial_baf_prauc}--\ref{tab:factorial_baf_f2}
present the full factorial results for ULB~2013 and BAF~Base, respectively.
The interaction heatmaps in
Figures~\ref{fig:heatmap_ulb_prauc}--\ref{fig:heatmap_ulb_f2} (ULB)
and Figures~\ref{fig:heatmap_baf_prauc}--\ref{fig:heatmap_baf_f2} (BAF)
visualise the per-model deltas.
Several cross-dataset patterns emerge:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RUS is uniformly harmful.}
    On ULB~2013, random under-sampling discards $\approx$99.7\% of
    legitimate samples, degrading PR-AUC by $0.07$--$0.25$.
    On BAF~Base the losses are smaller (LR~$-0.006$, RF~$-0.023$) but
    still consistently negative.
  \item \textbf{ROS and Weights benefit boosting models.}
    On ULB, CatBoost+ROS gains $+1.2$\,pp~$F_2$; on BAF,
    CatBoost+Weights achieves the highest overall PR-AUC ($0.185$,
    $+0.6$\,pp over baseline), and CatBoost+ROS is a close second
    ($0.184$).
    LGBM follows the same pattern on both datasets.
  \item \textbf{SMOTE $\equiv$ SMOTE+Tomek.}
    Results are numerically identical for all four models on both datasets.
    Tomek-link cleaning adds no measurable improvement, consistent with the
    expectation that tree-based learners are insensitive to boundary noise.
  \item \textbf{RF is uniquely hurt by oversampling.}
    On ULB no strategy improves upon the RF baseline ($F_2 = 0.862$);
    on BAF the degradation is severe: SMOTE drops PR-AUC from $0.154$
    to $0.111$ ($-28\%$ relative).
  \item \textbf{LR+Weights degrades on BAF.}
    While class-weighting is benign for boosting models, LR+Weights
    yields the worst PR-AUC on BAF ($0.118$), likely due to a
    convergence issue with the \texttt{saga} solver under extreme
    class reweighting on the harder task.
  \item \textbf{Overall, gains remain marginal.}
    When proper evaluation is used (PR-AUC scoring, $F_2$-optimised
    threshold), the benefit of resampling largely vanishes on both
    benchmarks.
    The largest absolute improvement across both datasets is
    CatBoost+Weights on BAF with $+0.6$\,pp PR-AUC.
\end{itemize}

(0.46 vs.\ 0.12--0.24) confirm that linear models struggle to exploit
the non-linear PCA feature interactions present in ULB.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Internal:} Hyperparameter tuning budget is finite
    (grid search, not Bayesian optimisation); single holdout split rather
    than repeated random sub-sampling; reliance on two dataset families.
  \item \textbf{External:} ULB features are PCA-anonymised, limiting
    interpretability and real-world applicability of ULB-specific findings.
    BAF is synthetically generated, so fraud patterns may not fully
    reflect real-world complexity.
  \item \textbf{Construct:} PR-AUC is sensitive to the class prior;
    comparisons of absolute PR-AUC values across datasets with different
    fraud rates should be made with caution.
  \item \textbf{Statistical:} Bootstrap CIs assume the holdout test set
    is representative; with extreme imbalance, bootstrap samples may
    have very few positive examples, widening CIs.
\end{itemize}
